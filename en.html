Why Islam?

For anyone looking for honest, rational answers before making a decision — a summary of the evidence Muslims rely on, presented without exaggeration:

◆ Fitrah: an inner sense that there is a Creator

By their very nature, human beings sense that this existence has a Creator. This shows clearly in hard moments, when the heart turns — without thinking — to the One it calls upon for help and relies on. The Qur'an describes this innate sense (fitrah) in these words:

فِطْرَتَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي فَطَرَ النَّاسَ عَلَيْهَا

[Adhere to] the fitrah of Allah upon which He has created [all] people.

Surah al-Rum (30), verse 30
Saheeh International translation of the meaning — for the original Arabic and other verified translations, see quran.com/30/30

◆ Who brought this universe into existence?

Everything we observe around us began at some point; it has not always existed. And whatever has a beginning must have a cause that brought it into being. The chain of causes cannot go back forever, so there must be a First who exists by Himself, who did not begin and was not created — the One who brought everything else into existence. This is a rational argument that Muslim scholars have used for centuries (such as al-Kindi and al-Ghazali), and in Western thought today it is known as the “cosmological argument” (the kalam argument).

◆ The astonishing precision of the laws of the universe

The physical laws and constants on which the universe rests (the strength of gravity, the proportions of the elements, the rate of expansion…) are so precise that even a tiny difference in them would have made life completely impossible. This extremely fine balance points strongly to deliberate design by a Wise, All-Knowing Creator — not to random chance.

◆ The Qur'an: a text preserved without alteration

The Qur'an was revealed more than 1,400 years ago, and since the time of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (peace and blessings be upon him) it has been preserved in two parallel ways: in writing, and in the memories of millions of Muslims who learned it by heart, generation after generation, through an unbroken chain of transmission. Any change to a single letter would be noticed immediately by millions of memorizers around the world — a level of preservation that, historically, no other religious text has achieved.

◆ Points in the Qur'an that invite reflection

Many callers to Islam point to passages in the Qur'an that agree with facts that were only discovered scientifically in modern times. Some of them, offered for reflection:

  • The stages of the embryo (al-Hajj 22:5; al-Muʾminun 23:12–14) — a precise, sequential description (nutfah, a drop; ʿalaqah, a clinging form; mudghah, a chewed-like lump) centuries before modern embryology.
  • The origin of iron (al-Hadid 57:25) — “And We sent down iron.” Astronomers explain that iron is actually formed inside massive stars and reaches the earth through meteorites, which fits the word “sent down” rather than “created.”
  • The expansion of the universe (al-Dhariyat 51:47) — “And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander,” which agrees with the twentieth-century discovery that the universe is expanding.

These are points for reflection that strengthen conviction — not a replacement for the basic rational and innate evidence above, and not “conclusive scientific proof” in the usual sense.

The Meaning

What does Islam mean?

Islam is submission to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, and yielding to Him by doing what He commands and leaving what He forbids. It is complying with Allah by affirming His Oneness (tawhid) and devoting worship sincerely to Him alone, and to no one else.

Part of this submission is: performing the prayer, giving zakah (obligatory charity), fasting Ramadan, making pilgrimage (Hajj) to the Sacred House, being dutiful to one's parents, keeping the ties of kinship, and abandoning all sins.

The religion of Allah is called Islam because it is humbling oneself before Allah, complying with obedience to Him, and leaving disobedience to Him — a submission that comes from humility before Allah and from longing for the reward and pleasure that are with Him.

إِنَّ الدِّينَ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ الْإِسْلَامُ

Indeed, the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam.

Surah Al ʿImran (3), verse 19

The Six Pillars of Faith

As they came in the hadith of Jibril (Gabriel), peace be upon him (hadith no. 2 of the Forty Hadith below, reported by Muslim), when the Prophet ﷺ was asked about faith (iman) and said: “That you believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day, and that you believe in divine decree (qadar), its good and its bad.”

1
Belief in AllahIn His existence; that He alone is the Lord and Creator, who alone deserves to be worshipped; and in His names and attributes.
2
Belief in the angelsCreated beings among the creation of Allah, created from light, who worship Him and never disobey what He commands them.
3
Belief in the booksThat Allah revealed to His messengers, such as the Torah, the Gospel (Injil) and the Psalms (Zabur), the last of which is the Holy Qur'an.
4
Belief in the messengersWhom Allah sent as bringers of good news and as warners, the last of whom is Muhammad ﷺ.
5
Belief in the Last DayResurrection after death, the reckoning, and recompense with Paradise or Hellfire.
6
Belief in divine decree, its good and its badThat Allah knows all things, has written them, willed them and created them.

The Three Levels of the Religion

From the same hadith it becomes clear that the religion has three levels, each higher than the one before it:

Islam

The outward deeds: the two testimonies, prayer, zakah, fasting and Hajj.

Iman (Faith)

Inner belief in the six pillars above.

Ihsan (Excellence)

“That you worship Allah as though you see Him; and although you do not see Him, He surely sees you.”

Stories from the Qur'an

Brief summaries of the stories of some of the prophets as told in the Holy Qur'an, with the places where each original story appears. Tap any story to read it.

Adam — peace be upon him▸

Allah created Adam from dust, breathed into him of the spirit He created, and taught him the names of all things. Then He commanded the angels to prostrate to him, and they all prostrated except Iblis (Satan), who was arrogant and refused, so Allah expelled him from His mercy. Allah let Adam and his wife Hawwaʾ (Eve) dwell in Paradise and forbade them to eat from one particular tree. Satan whispered to them until they ate from it; then they regretted it and turned to Allah in repentance, and He accepted their repentance and sent them down to the earth, to be its first successors (stewards).

Noah (Nuh) — peace be upon him▸

Allah sent him to his people to call them to worship Allah alone and to abandon the worship of idols. He remained among them for a thousand years less fifty, calling them night and day, yet only a few of them believed. Allah commanded him to build an ark, and he carried in it those who believed with him, and a pair of every kind of creature. Then Allah sent the flood, which drowned the disbelievers, and Noah and those with him in the ark were saved by the mercy of Allah.

Abraham (Ibrahim) — peace be upon him▸

Abraham grew up among a people who worshipped idols and heavenly bodies. He called them to the Oneness of Allah with argument and proof, and he broke their idols. His people tried to burn him as punishment, but Allah saved him from the fire unharmed. Allah commanded him to build the Kaʿbah together with his son Ishmael (Ismaʿil), peace be upon them both. He was tested with Allah's command to sacrifice his son, and when he submitted to his Lord's command, Allah ransomed the son with a great sacrifice in his place.

Joseph (Yusuf) — peace be upon him▸

Joseph saw a dream, and his brothers envied him because of it. They threw him into a well and left him there. Some travellers pulled him out, and he was sold as a slave in Egypt. There, the wife of his master tried to seduce him, but he refused and Allah protected him. He was unjustly imprisoned for years; then he interpreted the king's dreams, was released, and the king put him in charge of the storehouses of Egypt because of his trustworthiness and good management. In the end, Allah reunited him with his father and brothers after long years of separation and sorrow.

Moses (Musa) — peace be upon him▸

Moses was born at a time when Pharaoh was ordering the killing of the sons of the Children of Israel. Allah inspired his mother to place him in a chest and cast it into the river, and Pharaoh's household picked him up, so he was raised in Pharaoh's palace. Allah sent him as a messenger to Pharaoh, calling him to the Oneness of Allah and to let the Children of Israel go with him. Allah showed many signs at his hands, and parted the sea to save the Children of Israel, who crossed it; and Allah drowned Pharaoh and his soldiers when they followed them into it.

Jonah (Yunus) — peace be upon him▸

After calling his people for a long time, Jonah became angry with them and left them before Allah had permitted him to do so. He boarded a ship at sea, and the great fish (whale) swallowed him, and he was in its belly in layers of darkness. He called upon Allah in repentance, admitting his mistake, glorifying Allah with His praise and affirming His Oneness. Allah answered his prayer and saved him from the belly of the fish; then he returned to his people, and they all believed in him.

Jesus (ʿIsa), son of Mary — peace be upon him▸

The angels gave Mary (Maryam) — the righteous woman whom Allah had chosen and purified — the good news of a son whose name would be “the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary.” She said: “My Lord, how will I have a child when no man has touched me?” The answer came: “Such is Allah; He creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, ‘Be,’ and it is.” So Jesus was born without a father, by the power of Allah alone. The Qur'an makes this easier to understand with the example of Adam, whom Allah created from dust with neither father nor mother (Al ʿImran 3:59).

When she brought him to her people and they accused her, she pointed to him while he was still in the cradle, and Allah made him speak to clear his mother's name. The first words he said were: “Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet” (Maryam 19:30) — so the very first way he described himself was as a servant of Allah.

Allah gave him the Gospel (Injil), containing guidance and light, and supported him with great miracles — every one of which he connected to the permission of Allah, not to any power of his own: “I design for you from clay [that which is] like the form of a bird, then I breathe into it and it becomes a bird by permission of Allah. And I cure the blind and the leper, and I give life to the dead — by permission of Allah” (Al ʿImran 3:49). The same meaning is repeated in al-Maʾidah 5:110 with the words “by My permission.” The miracle is the act of Allah, and Jesus is a messenger through whose hands it takes place.

His call to the Children of Israel was the Oneness of God: “Indeed, Allah is my Lord and your Lord, so worship Him. That is the straight path” (Al ʿImran 3:51). And he gave good news of a messenger to come after him: “…and bringing good tidings of a messenger to come after me, whose name is Ahmad” (al-Saff 61:6). The disciples believed in him and said: “We believe in Allah and testify that we are Muslims [submitting to Him]” (Al ʿImran 3:52). Others rejected him and wanted to kill him, but Allah saved him: “And they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to resemble him to them… Rather, Allah raised him to Himself” (al-Nisaʾ 4:157–158).

The Qur'an describes a scene in which Allah asks Jesus: “Did you say to the people, ‘Take me and my mother as deities besides Allah’?” And Jesus himself declares his innocence: “Exalted are You! It was not for me to say that to which I have no right… I said not to them except what You commanded me — to worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord” (al-Maʾidah 5:116–117). He was never too proud to be a servant of Allah (al-Nisaʾ 4:172), and “it is not appropriate for the Most Merciful that He should take a son” (Maryam 19:92).

And so Jesus and Muhammad ﷺ meet in one single message: their Lord is One — Allah; both are servants of Allah and His messengers; and both called to the worship of Allah alone, without partner. This is the meaning of “Islam” in the Qur'an: submission to Allah alone, the religion of all the prophets: “He has ordained for you of religion what He enjoined upon Noah and that which We have revealed to you, and what We enjoined upon Abraham and Moses and Jesus — to establish the religion and not be divided therein” (al-Shura 42:13). That is why a Muslim believes in Jesus, loves him and honors him, saying: “We make no distinction between any of them, and we are Muslims [submitting] to Him” (Al ʿImran 3:84). And the Prophet ﷺ said: “I am the closest of people to Jesus, son of Mary, in this world and the Hereafter. The prophets are brothers of one father; their mothers are different, but their religion is one” (reported by al-Bukhari).

The Forty Hadith of al-Nawawi

A collection of comprehensive hadiths (sayings of the Prophet ﷺ) that sum up the foundations of the religion, chosen by Imam Muhyi al-Din Yahya ibn Sharaf al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him (d. 676 AH). It is known as “the Forty Hadith of al-Nawawi,” although most editions in circulation contain 42 hadiths (al-Hafiz Ibn Rajab later added two to complete it). Each hadith here is given with its narrator and its source as they appear in the standard editions. Tap any hadith to read it.

1Actions are by intentions▸

“Actions are only by intentions, and every person will have only what he intended. So whoever emigrated for Allah and His Messenger, his emigration is for Allah and His Messenger; and whoever emigrated for some worldly gain or to marry a woman, his emigration is for what he emigrated for.”

Narrated by ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab

2Islam, Iman and Ihsan (the hadith of Jibril)▸

“While we were sitting with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ one day, a man appeared before us whose clothes were extremely white and whose hair was extremely black; no sign of travel could be seen on him, and none of us knew him. He sat down by the Prophet ﷺ, rested his knees against his knees, and said: ‘O Muhammad, tell me about Islam.’ The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: ‘Islam is that you testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establish the prayer, give the zakah, fast Ramadan, and make pilgrimage to the House if you are able to.’ He said: ‘You have spoken truly.’ He said: ‘Then tell me about Iman (faith).’ He said: ‘That you believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day, and that you believe in divine decree, its good and its bad.’ He said: ‘You have spoken truly.’ He said: ‘Then tell me about Ihsan (excellence).’ He said: ‘That you worship Allah as though you see Him; and although you do not see Him, He surely sees you.’ Then he left, and the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘O ʿUmar, do you know who the questioner was?’ I said: ‘Allah and His Messenger know best.’ He said: ‘That was Jibril (Gabriel); he came to teach you your religion.’”

Frequently Asked Questions

Brief answers to questions that many seekers of truth ask before making their decision:

If God is merciful, why is there evil and suffering in the world?▸

Allah created human beings with real will and choice — not as programmed machines — and this choice is what makes good and evil possible in the first place. Much of the pain in this world is a test that reveals what people are truly made of, and it is followed by a just recompense in the Hereafter in which no one's right is lost. In this sense, this world is a passage to the final recompense, not the recompense itself.

Why can't I see God, so that I can believe in Him?▸

Many realities whose existence we do not doubt cannot be seen directly with our eyes (such as gravity and electricity); we recognize them by their effects. The existence of Allah is recognized through His effects — the precision of His creation and the order of the universe — not through direct sight in this life.

Does science contradict belief in God?▸

Experimental science explains how things work according to fixed laws, but it has no answer to why the universe came into existence from nothing in the first place, nor to where those laws themselves come from. Many great scientists throughout history, and still today, believe in a Creator — so there is no contradiction between seeking knowledge and believing in God.

What if I look into all this and then find out I was wrong?▸

Reflecting on where human beings come from and where they are going is a right that the mind owes to itself, and there is no loss in it as such. But if this evidence is indeed true, the greatest loss would be to ignore it without serious consideration — which is why these questions deserve enough time for honest reflection, not a hasty decision in either direction.

How to Become Muslim

Entering Islam does not require any rituals or any intermediary — only sincere belief in the heart together with pronouncing the two testimonies (the Shahadah):

أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله، وأشهد أن محمدًا رسول الله

Ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah

“I bear witness that there is none worthy of worship in truth except Allah alone, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.”

1Sincere belief in the heart in the Oneness of Allah, and that Muhammad ﷺ is His true Messenger to all of humanity.
2Pronouncing the two testimonies above — with this, a person becomes Muslim immediately, and all previous sins are forgiven.
3Learning the rest of the teachings of the religion gradually afterwards (prayer, rulings…) — you are not required to complete all of this at once.
